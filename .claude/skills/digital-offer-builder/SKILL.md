# Digital Offer Builder

Built from the Sustainable Scholars Digital Product Offer Playbook (Nov 2026). Full research, benchmarks and sources: project file `digital-products/digital-product-offer-playbook.md`.

## When to use
- "Turn this idea into a product / offers", "how should I price this", "build me an offer ladder"
- "I need $X by [date]", "launch plan for my course / membership / guide"
- Designing bumps, upsells, downsells, bundles, guarantees or a launch sequence

## Before you start: ask only what changes the plan
If not already known, ask (in one message): revenue target and deadline, budget, hours per day, audience you can already reach (list size, followers, warm contacts), formats the person is willing to deliver, and any price they already charge. Proceed on stated assumptions if they are away.

## Steps

1. **Market gate.** Score 1–5 each: pain, buying power, reachable, growing/urgent. Need 14/20 to proceed. Market beats offer beats copy.
2. **Dream outcome** in the buyer's words: "[Who] gets [result] in [time] without [what they dread]."
3. **Obstacles → solutions.** List every obstacle before, during and after; flip each into a solution.
4. **Delivery matrix.** For each solution, list versions at each level: DIY info (free–$49), DIY tool/AI ($19–199 or $9–29/mo), group done-with-you ($197–997), 1:1 ($500+), done-for-you, B2B license. Cross with format and unit (one item, one season, one cohort).
5. **Trim and stack.** Cut high-cost/low-value pieces. Keep low-cost/high-value items (templates, checklists, data cuts, AI prompts) as bonuses.
6. **Build the ladder:** free content → lead magnet → front-end → core → continuity → premium group → high-touch → B2B.
7. **Checkout stack (always):** one order bump (20–40% of main price, completes the purchase), one one-click upsell (impulse ≤25%, or a premium next step at 2–3x), one downsell matched to the "no" reason (payment plan, lite version, trial, smaller unit). Never more than 2–3 post-purchase offers. Never make the downsell a better deal than the original.
8. **Price** between the value ceiling, the next-best alternative, and the cost floor. Aim near 1/10 of credible value. Use anchoring, a decoy middle tier, charm prices low and round prices high. Default to a founding price with a real deadline for a new offer. Don't cut prices to fix sales; fix reach or the offer.
9. **Guarantee and name.** Risk reversal written plainly and honored exactly. Name = outcome + mechanism + audience + time.
10. **Revenue math table.** Show minimum, realistic and stretch scenarios as units × price for each rung, and the take rates assumed. Plan conservatively: bump 25–30%, upsell 10–15%, downsell 10%. Show fees (about 2.9% + 30¢ on Stripe). State the plain goal ("8 buyers and 1 cohort seat").
11. **Unit economics** if ads are involved: AOV, CAC, 12-month LTV, LTV:CAC (target 3:1), payback. If it doesn't work, lead with owned and warm channels.
12. **Launch plan, day by day**, sized to the person's hours and budget: pre-sell or soft launch to warm contacts first, three prelaunch content pieces (opportunity, transformation, ownership), a 4–7 day open cart with a real close, last-call emails on the final day (where many sales land), then deliver and invite buyers to the next rung. Use real calendar moments (holidays, seasonal deadlines).
13. **Risks and fixes**: no sales in 48 hours (reach problem: DMs to warm contacts), objections, capacity limits.

## Premium tier (when asked "can we charge $X at the top?")
1. **Comparables first:** what the next-best option costs (for example, private college counselors average $239/hr and about $6,400 per student; packages $3,763–$7,886, per a 2025 IEC pricing survey). Then the value math (one result × years). The price should be a small fraction of both.
2. **Justify the jump with access, not content.** A 4–5x step over the group tier needs personal help: 1:1 sessions, done-for-you deliverables, edits or reviews, direct messaging, and a follow-up at the next pain point.
3. **Cap seats to fit the person's hours** (hours per client × seats ≤ available hours). The cap is the scarcity.
4. **Guarantee the work and accuracy, not the outcome** (for example, "N applications submitted or we keep working free"), with a clear list of what the client has to do. Add a short refund window after kickoff.
5. **Proof:** 2–3 real results with numbers, plus a sample deliverable. Without proof, sell it openly as a founding cohort at a lower founding price in exchange for feedback and a testimonial.
6. **Say who it's for and who it's not for.**
7. **Sell it with apply + a 20-minute call**, not a checkout button. Offer a payment plan with a premium of about 10%.
8. **Show three tiers side by side**; the premium tier anchors the page and makes the middle tier the obvious pick.
9. **Honest forecast:** warm lists typically convert 1–3% to high-ticket buyers when there's a call step (assumption). Show month-one math both with and without payment plans.
10. **Validate cheaply:** a Van Westendorp price survey (too cheap / bargain / expensive / too expensive), a refundable deposit pre-sell, and close rate on the first 10 calls (more than half say yes = underpriced, raise for the next seats). Use sequential price steps instead of A/B tests on small lists.

## Expanding revenue without much more work (reuse-first)
1. **Inventory first.** Before inventing anything, list what already exists: live plans and prices, registered users and lists, automations (intake → report), recorded sessions, data sets, waitlists, booking tools. Check the actual site or code, not memory.
2. **Reuse what's built over making something new.** If a membership already exists, use it as the downsell rather than creating a new one. Flag tiers that overlap (for example, a monthly 1:1 plan vs. a premium program) and decide which to lead with.
3. **Generate candidates from these patterns:** annual or season prepay of existing plans (with a holiday deadline); bundles of existing pieces (20–25% off the parts total); sibling/family add-on; gift cards (December); counselor/organization licenses of existing lists or plans; a one-time unlock of an existing automated product; recordings of live sessions sold as replays and used as bonuses; seasonal kits from premium deliverables (e.g., appeal letters in spring); referral and affiliate commissions; waitlist products.
4. **For each candidate give:** price, what it reuses, new hours, expected revenue (with assumed take rates on the real audience size), and $ per new hour. Rank by $ per new hour. Note the season and the sales cycle (B2B is slow but renews).
5. **Discount for overlap** (the same buyers appear in several offers) and show low and expected cases.
6. **Exclude** offers that consume the person's limited live hours unless priced for it.
7. **Build order:** do the top-ranked, under-an-hour items before the launch date; schedule the rest by season.

## Best-offer principles (plain language)
Sell the result, not the stuff. Value = big result × belief it will happen ÷ (time × effort). Build the offer from the buyer's obstacles. Charge for attention. Higher prices usually mean more profit. Always offer a next step. Show three choices. Remove risk without overpromising. Make scarcity real. Proof beats persuasion. Say who it's not for. Sell before you build.

## Rules
- Label every number as sourced, standard practice, or an assumption.
- No outcome or income promises; testimonials must be real, typical or labeled, with relationships disclosed; refunds as written; subscriptions need clear terms, consent and easy cancellation (FTC/ROSCA).
- For minors' data, collect only what's needed with parent consent.
- Prefer a concierge (manual, AI-assisted) version before building software.
- Sell the next rung at the moment of delivery of the current one.

## Output format
1. One-line offer summary and the plain revenue goal
2. Offer array table (piece, what it is, price now/later, format)
3. Revenue math table
4. Budget table
5. Day-by-day launch plan
6. Risks and fixes
7. Assumptions to confirm

## Benchmarks (vendor-reported; order of magnitude only)
- Order bump take rate 30–40%; one-time offer about 23%; email-sequence upsell about 11% (systeme.io; Focus Digital 2026).
- Bumps + upsells raised creator AOV about 129%; repeat buyers about 12% of purchases but 44% of revenue; $499+ products 5% of transactions, 55% of revenue (SamCart 2023).
- Course completion: scheduled cohorts 53%, self-paced 42%; cohorts of 11–25 complete best (Ruzuku 2026).
- Email: median open 43%, click 2.1% (MailerLite 2025).
- LTV:CAC 3:1 healthy, below 1:1 loses money (Chargebee).
